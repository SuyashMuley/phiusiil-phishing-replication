## C. PhiUSIIL leakage / shortcut evidence
- No paper found whose stated aim is auditing PhiUSIIL label leakage. Closest: Rahman et al. 2026 (G-Tech), which shows two features (URLSimilarityIndex Pearson r=0.8604; HasSocialNet r=0.7843) give 99.63% accuracy and a single AND gate 99.67%. That paper presents this as interpretability and "sufficient for near-perfect classification"; the word "leakage" is my inference, not theirs. URLSimilarityIndex is a feature the PhiUSIIL authors engineered from the label-bearing crawl, so this is a plausible shortcut, but it is not proven in any source I read.
- Ahamed et al. 2026 show PhiUSIIL-trained URL models collapse to the phishing-prevalence floor (~0.432) under subdomain injection and have 0.25-0.54 FPR on Majestic Million benign sites.
- Arp et al. 2022 supplies the general framework (spurious correlations, sampling bias) but does not mention PhiUSIIL.

## D. Claims from the plans that were wrong or unsupported
1. "PhiUSIIL headline accuracy ~99.2% (single number)": NOT supported. Only snippet seen is precision 99.79% (pre-training) / 99.24% (incremental). Split type unknown. 99.24% also equals a different paper's MLP accuracy.
2. Frontiers paper: numbers are largely right, but "cross-source 0.73-0.87" applies only to the dual-source test (and only to LR, XGBoost, BERT as far as I could see). The PhishTank-phishing-only external test shows 0.995-0.999, which is not meaningful cross-source evidence. "Host-disjoint" label not confirmed in the abstract.
3. Rashid et al.: "same-dataset F1 0.98-1.00" and "cross-dataset 0.67-0.94" UNVERIFIED. Only the +0.06 average (up to ~0.2) UDA gain is confirmed.
4. Misiek & Hyla: 77.84% accuracy ~ consistent with abstract's 77.8%; F1 77.93% UNVERIFIED. Also PhiUSIIL is one of the three test sets, and the model uses hand-crafted features.
5. Sabir et al.: real title is "Reliability and Robustness analysis ..." (IEEE TDSC), not literally "adversarial robustness of ML phishing URL detectors". Substance is fine.
6. Excluded as misattributed candidate: BDCC "Fragility of Phishing Detection Models" is about email corpora (CEAS_08, Enron, etc.), not URLs, and does not use PhiUSIIL.

## E. Summary: in-distribution vs cross-source (URL-only models)
Best credible in-distribution URL-only results are around 99.6-99.8% accuracy: Ahamed et al. 2026 report 0.9962-0.9984 on PhiUSIIL test sets for LR/XGBoost/CharCNN/BERT (abstract-verified), and a 2-feature ANN reaches 99.63% on PhiUSIIL, which indicates PhiUSIIL is nearly separable by a shortcut feature, so these numbers say little about real difficulty. Best credible cross-source numbers are far lower and noisier: Ahamed et al. get 0.73-0.87 accuracy with 0.25-0.54 FPR when PhishTank phishing is paired with Majestic Million benign sites; Misiek & Hyla report 77.8% average accuracy over three datasets (XGBoost); Mia et al. get 51-59% accuracy when XGBoost is trained on one dataset and tested on another using 20 shared features (full text verified). Rashid et al. show domain adaptation recovers only ~0.06 F1 on average. Realistic expectation for a PhiUSIIL-trained URL-only model on an external benign/phish mix is therefore roughly 70-85% accuracy with high false positive rates, a drop of 15-25+ points from the ~99.7% in-distribution figure. Temporal phishing-only checks (Ozmen & Yildirim: 149/150 recalled) are less informative because benign false positives are not measured.
